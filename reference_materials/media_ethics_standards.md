# Media Literacy and Ethical Journalism Framework

This reference outlines standard professional ethics and critical framing patterns used to analyze the quality, objectivity, and biases of news coverage.

## 1. The SPJ Code of Ethics (Society of Professional Journalists)
*   **Seek Truth and Report It**: Journalists should be honest, fair, and courageous in gathering, reporting, and interpreting information.
*   **Minimize Harm**: Ethical journalists treat sources, subjects, and colleagues as human beings deserving of respect.
*   **Act Independently**: The highest and primary obligation of ethical journalism is to serve the public. Journalists should avoid conflicts of interest, corporate pressures, and political alignment.
*   **Be Accountable and Transparent**: Ethical journalism means taking responsibility for one's work and explaining one's decisions to the public.

## 2. Common Framing and Media Bias Patterns
*   **Sensationalism / Clickbait**: Exaggerating elements of a story to provoke emotional reactions (fear, anger, awe) at the expense of context.
*   **Selection Bias (Omission)**: Choosing to report certain details or stories while completely ignoring others to support a pre-existing narrative.
*   **False Equivalence**: Presenting two opposing viewpoints as equally valid or supported by evidence when they are not.
*   **Ad Hominem / Genetic Fallacy**: Focusing critique on the source of an argument rather than the substance of the argument itself.
*   **Straw Man Fallacy**: Distorting or oversimplifying an opponent's argument to make it easier to attack.
*   **Scare Tactics**: Using hyperbole or fear-inducing language to influence public opinion (e.g. framing regulation as "stifling all progress" or lack thereof as "civilization-ending").
