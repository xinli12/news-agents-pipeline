# Macroeconomic Indicators and Financial Policy Framework

This reference outlines key economic data points, metrics, and models used to assess market conditions and the financial impact of policy decisions.

## 1. Key Economic Indicators
*   **Consumer Price Index (CPI)**: Measures the average change over time in the prices paid by urban consumers for a market basket of consumer goods and services. Cite the **Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) CPI-U (CPI for All Urban Consumers)** monthly release.
*   **Producer Price Index (PPI)**: Measures the average change over time in the selling prices received by domestic producers for their output. Cite the **BLS PPI for Software Publishers (Series PCU511210511210)** or **Internet Publishing and Broadcasting (Series PCU519130519130)** as early indicators of tech supply-chain cost trends.
*   **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**: The total monetary or market value of all the finished goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period. Cite the **Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) Digital Economy Satellite Account** report (which measures the digital economy's percentage contribution to US GDP, such as the 10% benchmark) or the quarterly **BEA Real GDP Release**.
*   **Federal Funds Rate**: The target interest rate set by the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) at which commercial banks borrow and lend excess reserves overnight. Influences all other interest rates.
*   **Unemployment Rate (U-3/U-6)**: The percentage of the total labor force that is unemployed but actively seeking employment and willing to work.

## 2. Economic Assessment Models
*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA)**: A systematic process for calculating and comparing benefits and costs of a project, decision, or government regulation.
*   **Compliance Costs / Barriers to Entry**: Expenses incurred by firms in meeting government regulations. High compliance costs act as barriers to entry, benefiting large incumbents over small startups.
*   **Positive/Negative Externalities**: Costs or benefits of an economic activity experienced by unrelated third parties (e.g., pollution as a negative externality; education as a positive externality).
